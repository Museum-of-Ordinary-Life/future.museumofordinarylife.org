TheMuseum of Ordinary Life
VisitNetworkVirtual museumArchiveContributeAbout

What used to be ordinary is now one of the hardest things to explain.

The Museum preserves everyday life across periods of enormous change: the things people touched, waited for, paid for, repaired, carried, complained about, depended on and eventually stopped needing.

The collection began in 2026 with a simple instruction: document what you would normally never think to document.

The Museum now exists wherever the collection can be encountered: buildings, neighborhoods, shared archives and virtual space.

Museum information

The museum changed because ordinary life changed.

Admission

There isn’t any. Walk in. If a room is full, the site will say so.

Food

Every location has a common kitchen. Eat there whether or not you came for the museum.

Stewardship

Locations are cared for by rotating local stewardship groups, not permanent executive staff.

Collections

Records connect through a shared archive. Physical objects can remain with the communities that hold their context; exhibitions travel when people choose to share them.

Store

There is no museum shop. Catalogs, scans and reproducible objects are available to make or take as needed.
