Museum information / About

A museum for the things we haven’t lost yet.

The Museum of Ordinary Life is a living archive of everyday human life, established in 2026 while the material it collects is still ordinary.

What we do

We document ordinary objects, spaces, interfaces, routines, sounds, paperwork and personal accounts that usually pass through the world without entering a historical record.

The Museum is interested not only in what something looked like, but in how it fit into a life: where it lived, how it was used, what systems surrounded it and what the person living with it thought about it.

Why 2026?

Because this is when we started.

Historical archives are often assembled after the fact, when the mundane details of an era have already disappeared. We are trying the opposite: documenting the present before we know what will eventually seem important, strange, obsolete, beautiful or difficult to explain.

What counts?

A grocery receipt can count. So can a bus stop, a work badge, a phone notification, a medicine cabinet, a rent notice, a junk drawer, a five-minute recording of a street, or the place somebody charges their phone every night.

Rarity is not a requirement. Neither is beauty. Neither is a profound story. Ubiquity may be more historically useful than uniqueness.

What kind of museum is this?

The Museum is a distributed network rather than a single institution: shared physical hubs, local and community museums, temporary exhibitions, distributed collections, shared archives and virtual spaces. Its collection can originate anywhere ordinary life is being lived and documented.

No location has to become the authoritative account of a place or community. Several museums can overlap, disagree, remain partly offline, or connect only selected records to the wider network.

It remains part public-history project, part participatory archive, part artwork, and part experiment in noticing one period from another.
