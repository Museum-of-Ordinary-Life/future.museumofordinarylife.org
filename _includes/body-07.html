The project has since expanded beyond a book into a broader experiment in worldbuilding, archival practice, art, design, software, social imagination and the creation of real artifacts from a fictional future.

Why make the Museum real?

In the fictional future, the Museum helps people understand how ordinary life worked before and during the transition. Rather than inventing that historical collection later, the real Museum begins collecting now—while the world it will someday remember is still the present.

A real photograph made in 2026 can therefore function simultaneously as contemporary documentation, an artwork, historical evidence, and an artifact inside the fictional continuity.

The reality layer

The Museum is the first part of a wider “Reality Layer”: websites, archives, tools, interfaces, documents, institutions and other fragments of the imagined future that can exist meaningfully in the present.

Some may remain fictional artifacts. Some may become functioning prototypes. Some may become genuinely useful institutions independent of the story that inspired them.

The rule

Blur ontology, not consent.

The project may play with whether an artifact belongs to the present or the future, whether something began as art or institution, and where fiction ends and ordinary life begins. It shouldn’t create ambiguity around safety, privacy, money, identity, emergencies, official authority or other consequential real-world facts.

Why hide this here?

Because the Museum should be able to stand on its own.

A visitor doesn't need to know about the novel—or even care about fiction—to contribute something meaningful to the archive. The deeper context is here for people who go looking for it.

Working method / prefigurative fiction

Don’t merely describe a possible future. Build small pieces of it in the present, then let what happens teach you something about the future you were imagining.
