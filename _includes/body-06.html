Everyone can. Contributors don't need archival training or a theory of why something matters. The person living with an object often knows something about it that a curator never could.

The Museum preserves contributors’ own words wherever possible and distinguishes archival originals from edited public copies.

Who is behind the Museum?

The Museum began in 2026 with inaugural contributors. It is now stewarded across a distributed network rather than governed from a permanent institutional center.

Local stewardship groups care for collections, preservation, access, interpretation and exhibitions. The archive remains shared across communities; labels can be revised, disputed or supplemented without waiting for a single central curator to authorize the change.

Technical systems can assist with preservation, cataloging, duplication, translation and access. Human judgment remains central where context, consent, interpretation, memory and disagreement are involved.

Consent travels with the record

Public display, research, machine learning, generative reuse, attribution, delayed release and unknown future uses remain separate permissions. A contribution can be preserved without becoming public, and becoming public does not make it unrestricted data.

Provenance, contributor wording and stated limits are part of the historical record. When a use cannot be reversed technically, the Museum says so instead of pretending control is absolute.

What are we trying to preserve?

Context.

The invisible systems underneath an ordinary Tuesday: money, work, housing, technology, transportation, bureaucracy, domestic routines, social rules, waiting, maintenance, care, convenience and inconvenience.

People rarely experience these things as “history” while they are happening. That is exactly why we are interested in them.

The question that started the Museum

In 2026: if someone fifty years from now wanted to understand what it felt like to be alive then, what would we wish we had saved?

Project context / beneath the museum

This museum exists in two times at once.

The Museum of Ordinary Life is a real archive established in 2026. This site is a speculative future version of that institution: one possible interface for the Museum after the world around it has changed. The Museum also exists inside the larger speculative-fiction project No One Noticed.

The larger project

No One Noticed began as a speculative-fiction project about a near-future societal and technological transformation that happens so gradually—and so seamlessly— that the people living through it don't recognize a singular moment when the world changes.
